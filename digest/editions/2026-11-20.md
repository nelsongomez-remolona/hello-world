## 1. The Fed came close to hiking in October and expects to in December

**What happened.** Minutes of the Fed's 27–28 October meeting, [released 18 Nov](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/files/fomcminutes20261028.pdf), show that the decision to hold at 3.75–4.00% was a close call. Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack [dissented in favor of a 25bp hike](https://www.newsquawk.com/headlines/primer-fomc-minutes-due-wednesday-18th-november-at-1900gmt1400est). According to [AP](https://www.adn.com/business-economy/2026/11/18/fed-minutes-higher-interest-rates-needed-in-december-as-inflation-worries-mount/), nearly all officials expect a quarter-point hike in December, and two raised the possibility of a half point. The S&P 500 [fell for a fifth straight session](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/usa/how-major-us-stock-indexes-fared-wednesday-11182026-nid18112026/) on 18 Nov, its longest losing streak since June.

**Background.** The Fed's rate-setting committee (the FOMC) publishes minutes three weeks after each meeting. They show how widely views differed in the room, which the short statement released on decision day doesn't, so markets read them for the committee's next move.

**Why it matters.** The Fed is now moving the same way as the Bank of England and the RBA (yesterday's brief). Higher US rates raise borrowing costs worldwide through the dollar. One complication: Cook voted for a *tighter* policy while the White House is [trying to remove her](https://www.justsecurity.org/165038/early-edition-november-18-2026/), with a hearing on 14 Dec. That undercuts the claim that she is soft on inflation, and it makes it harder to argue the case against her is about monetary policy.

*Confidence:* the minutes are primary. The December expectation is AP's summary of them. · *Watch:* the December FOMC decision; US CPI.

## 2. Gulf: Iran says tankers hit mines east of Hormuz

**What happened.** Iran's navy [said three oil tankers caught fire after hitting mines](https://english.news.cn/20261119/1e486e6c7d604f2a8c861850e65f9c21/c.html) east of the Strait of Hormuz, in what it calls a "forbidden" zone, after the ships switched off their transponders and ignored warnings. Ship names and flags haven't been released, and [the claim has not been independently verified](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/iran-us-conflict/iran-says-3-oil-tankers-catch-fire-after-hitting-mines-east-of-strait-of-hormuz). Separately, Iranian officials [dismissed reports](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/iran-us-conflict/iran-says-3-oil-tankers-catch-fire-after-hitting-mines-east-of-strait-of-hormuz) of a plot to attack US officials in Washington with chemical weapons.

**Background.** Naval mines are cheap and hard to find, and clearing them takes weeks of specialist work, even after hostilities stop. In the 1980s Iran–Iraq "Tanker War," a single mine nearly sank the US frigate *Samuel B. Roberts* in 1988. Missiles are a threat only while someone keeps firing them. Mines remain a threat until someone removes them.

**Why it matters.** If the claim is true, it's a qualitative change from yesterday's picture. Insurers price *where* a ship may sail, not just whether fighting has stopped, and a mined zone keeps those premiums high after any ceasefire. *Skeptical reading:* Iran may be using the claim to deter traffic without having laid a large minefield. Unverified claims of mining can work almost as well as real ones.

*Confidence:* this is Iran's claim alone and unverified. · *Watch:* identification of the ships; any US or allied mine-clearing operation.

## 3. Ebola in Congo passes 11,600 cases, with no approved vaccine

**What happened.** The CDC [reports 11,645 confirmed cases and 5,683 deaths](https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/situation-summary/index.html) as of 17 Nov, nearly all in the Democratic Republic of Congo. Kenya has 9 cases, Uganda 20, and Nigeria and France one imported case each. WHO's head [said on 11 Nov](https://www.who.int/news-room/speeches/item/who-director-general-s-opening-remarks-at-the-member-state-briefing-on-ebola-outbreak-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo---11-november-2026) that more than 1,000 contacts have been lost to follow-up because of insecurity, and that the outbreak remains a public health emergency of international concern.

**Background.** Ebola is a family of viruses. The approved vaccine, Ervebo, was developed for the *Zaire* species. This outbreak is the *Bundibugyo* species, for which [no vaccine or treatment is approved](https://www.who.int/emergencies/disease-outbreak-news/item/2026-DON625). A trial of Ervebo began in October, and WHO advisers have recommended two vaccine candidates designed for Bundibugyo. Until then, contact tracing and isolation are the main tools, and both depend on health workers being able to reach people safely.

**Why it matters.** This is the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record, behind West Africa in 2014–16 (about 28,600 cases). Cases in Kenya, a regional air hub, raise the risk of international spread. For governments the decisions are funding for the response, travel screening, and whether to fast-track the candidate vaccines.

*Confidence:* CDC and WHO figures are primary. · *Watch:* weekly case counts; results of the vaccine trials.

## 4. Walmart: a raised outlook, and squeezed households

**What happened.** Walmart's third-quarter sales [rose 5.2% to $188.8bn](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/walmart-wmt-q3-2026-earnings.html). It [raised full-year guidance for the third time](https://www.abc15.com/business/company-news/walmart-raises-its-outlook-for-the-year-for-the-3rd-time-after-a-solid-quarter) to sales growth of 4.9–5.2% and earnings per share of $2.84–2.89. Online sales grew 17%. US comparable sales excluding fuel rose 2.9%. Walmart's slides, as [reported by Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/walmart-q3-fy27-slides-strong-results-raised-guidance-amid-costs-93CH-5039238), put the drag from lower prescription-drug prices at about 1.25 points. The CEO [told CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/walmart-wmt-q3-2026-earnings.html) the company aims to keep prices low despite sharply higher fuel costs.

**Background.** The prescription-drug hit comes from Medicare's drug-price negotiation. Since January, the government has set a "maximum fair price" for the first ten negotiated drugs, which lowers pharmacy revenue. Separately, after the Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs, Walmart [put about $2.9bn of tariff refunds into price cuts](https://www.tikr.com/blog/walmart-fell-10-after-its-last-report-is-it-time-to-sell-before-november-19-or-load-up) earlier this year.

**Why it matters.** Walmart serves a large share of US households, so its numbers are an early read on consumers. Online and grocery sales are growing, which suggests people are shopping more carefully, not spending more freely. Higher fuel costs from the Gulf war act like a tax on lower-income households, and the Fed is about to raise rates on top of that.

*Confidence:* the headline figures are confirmed. The drug-price drag comes from a secondary report of Walmart's slides. · *Watch:* holiday sales data.

## 5. The US and Venezuela restore military ties

**What happened.** On a surprise visit to Caracas on 18 Nov, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth [announced a deal to reestablish military ties](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/18/us-defence-secretary-announces-military-partnership-on-trip-to-venezuela) with the interim government of Delcy Rodríguez. He said it covers joint training and intelligence sharing. [AP reports](https://www.wbtv.com/2026/11/18/us-venezuela-sign-deal-reestablish-military-ties-hegseth-says-visit-interim-leaders/) that its terms, including any legal basis for US forces in Venezuela, haven't been disclosed. Hegseth described Venezuela as a "compliant security partner" since US forces seized Nicolás Maduro in a military operation on 3 January.

**Background.** Venezuela has the world's largest proven oil reserves. Military ties with the US were cut under Hugo Chávez in the 2000s, and Venezuela turned to Russia and China for arms. An oil deal in August came first. A status-of-forces agreement, the treaty that sets the legal rules for foreign troops in a country, was reportedly under negotiation in early November.

**Why it matters.** *Supporters' reading:* it secures a large non-Gulf oil supplier and pushes out Russian and Chinese military influence in the Western Hemisphere. *Critics' reading* ([cited by Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/18/us-defence-secretary-announces-military-partnership-on-trip-to-venezuela)): it makes Venezuela a client state under an unelected interim government. Which reading holds depends on whether and when elections are held.

*Confidence:* the deal is confirmed. Its terms are undisclosed. · *Watch:* the deal text or a status-of-forces agreement; an election timetable.

## Concept: case fatality ratio

The *case fatality ratio* (CFR) is deaths divided by confirmed cases. For this outbreak it is about 49% (5,683 ÷ 11,645). It's easy to misread in both directions. Early in an outbreak, many patients haven't recovered or died yet, so the CFR is *understated*. Mild cases often never get tested, which makes it *overstated*. **Ask what the denominator counts before comparing a CFR across outbreaks.**
