# The Daily Brief — Special Edition: Five Companies Worth a Deeper Look

*Thursday, 19 November 2026 · 7 minutes · Fact-checked: 23 claims checked, 4 corrected, 2 labelled ([log](../factchecks/2026-11-19-special.md))*

**What this is.** These are five companies where this month's biggest themes (Hormuz risk, energy-shock inflation, AI spending, AI-found software flaws, healthcare regulation) show up directly in the financials, *and* where serious people disagree about what comes next. The list isn't a recommendation to buy. It doesn't account for valuation relative to your goals, your existing holdings, or your time horizon. Each entry ends with the questions a deep dive should answer.

**Excluded:** Anthropic, because this brief is made with Claude, an Anthropic model, which is a conflict of interest. Also excluded are stocks that moved this week on momentum alone.

---

## 1. Nvidia (NVDA): strong numbers, a leadership shock

**Why now.** Fiscal Q3 revenue [rose 103% to $115.8bn](https://www.thestreet.com/latest-news/nvda-nvidia-corporation-q3-2027-earnings-stock-updates), well above the roughly $109bn consensus, and the stock fell anyway. CEO Jensen Huang [resigned on 12 Nov](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-CEO-Transition/) after reports that he had been the target of a foreign intelligence effort. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO, and the board [says it won't reinstate him](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/nvidia-interim-ceo-kress-addresses-staff-after-jensen-huang-exit-.html).

**Bull case.** Demand for AI computing hardware is still growing faster than supply, and Q4 guidance is [$130bn at the midpoint](https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/stocks/us-stocks/262309793-nvidia-earnings-report-q3-rubin-ramp-data-center-growth-revenue-guidance-gross-margin-tradingkey). A leadership shock at a company this far ahead can be a buying opportunity.

**Bear case.** The guidance covers a [14-week quarter](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/331079/20261119/nvidia-stock-slides-after-earnings-extra-week-makes-130b-guidance-4-growth-not-12.htm), so growth per week is about 4%, not 12%. Gross margin is guided down to [71–72%](https://www.thestreet.com/latest-news/nvda-nvidia-corporation-q3-2027-earnings-stock-updates). Growth is slowing and margins are falling at the same time, while the company's main technical and customer relationship-holder has left.

**Questions for the deep dive.** Are the lower margins temporary (costs of launching the new Rubin chips) or a sign of competition (custom chips from hyperscalers)? What do the big cloud customers' 2027 spending plans imply? Could the counterintelligence angle bring export-control or national-security scrutiny?

## 2. Cheniere Energy (LNG): Hormuz risk, converted into long-term contracts

**Why now.** Q3 revenue [rose 39% to $6.17bn](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000003570/000000357026000040/cei20263rdqtrerex991.htm). The company raised its 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance for a third straight quarter, to $8.75–9.25bn, and its dividend by more than 12%. It has bought back $1.8bn of shares this year. Most telling: **QatarEnergy, the world's largest LNG exporter, signed contracts to buy up to 4 million tonnes a year from Cheniere's US plants, [from the early 2030s through 2054](https://chemxplore.com/news/cheniere-third-quarter-results-raises-guidance)**. Coverage links the deals to the disruption of Qatar's own exports, which pass through Hormuz. One million tonnes of that depends on Cheniere deciding to go ahead with a planned expansion.

**Bull case.** This is the "risk premium that outlasts the risk" from today's Concept, turning into revenue. Buyers are paying for supply that doesn't pass through a chokepoint, and locking it in for decades. Cheniere's new trains are being completed and its credit rating was [upgraded to A-](https://chemxplore.com/news/cheniere-third-quarter-results-raises-guidance).

**Bear case.** Adjusted EPS of $3.62 [missed the $4.20 consensus](https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/3020965/), and costs nearly doubled, so EBITDA grew only 18%. A large wave of new LNG supply from Qatar, the US and Canada arrives later this decade. Some of this year's profits come from short-term spot sales, which will shrink if the Gulf calms down. Sources [disagree on total debt](https://www.panabee.com/news/cheniere-energy-earnings-q3-2026-report) ($18.5bn to $24bn depending on definition).

**Questions for the deep dive.** What share of volumes are sold on long-term fixed-fee contracts, and what share on the spot market? How much would earnings fall if prices returned to 2024 levels? When does the global supply wave arrive?

## 3. International Seaways (INSW): record profits at the top of the cycle

**Why now.** Q3 net income was [$344m, or $6.90 a share, against $71m a year earlier](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001679049/000110465926123058/tm2629068d1_ex99-1.htm). Its largest tankers (VLCCs) on the spot market [earned $154,597 a day, against $34,809](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001679049/000110465926123058/tm2629068d1_ex99-1.htm). The quarterly dividend is $5.05, the fourth straight quarter paying out at least 85% of adjusted earnings.

**Bull case.** CEO Lois Zabrocky argues demand will *rise* after Hormuz reopens, because OECD crude inventories have fallen by about [250 million barrels](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2900839-intl-seaways-expects-post-war-tanker-demand-surge) since the war began and will need restocking. (This is the company's own figure; I haven't found independent data confirming it.)

**Bear case.** Tanker earnings are among the most cyclical in any industry, and war premiums [can fade quickly](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/3365461/strait-of-hormuz-tensions-spike-tanker-trade-these-2-stocks-are-set-to-benefit). With an 85%+ payout ratio, the dividend falls as soon as day rates fall. The current price may already assume high rates continue.

**Questions for the deep dive.** What day rate does the share price imply? How many new tankers are on order versus scheduled for scrapping? How much of the fleet is locked in on fixed-rate time charters?

## 4. UnitedHealth (UNH): a regulatory repricing

**Why now.** The stock [fell about 10% on 18 Nov](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/price-unitedhealth-group-nyse-unh-stock-falls-102-heres-what-happened-2026-11-18/), and every large managed-care company fell with it. The trigger was a [CMS proposed rule released 16 Nov](https://www.risehealth.org/insights-articles/article/cms-crush-proposed-rule-medicare-advantage/). It would let Medicare extrapolate audit findings from a sample of patients to a plan's entire contract, going back to 2018. CMS estimates this would recover about $9bn a year.

**Background.** Medicare Advantage pays insurers more for sicker patients, based on the diagnoses the insurers record. Audits check whether patients' medical records support those diagnoses. A [2023 version of this rule was struck down](https://www.groom.com/resources/court-rules-that-cms-cannot-extrapolate-medicare-advantage-risk-adjustment-audit-results/) in 2025 after Humana sued, on procedural grounds, not on whether extrapolation itself is lawful.

**Bull case.** It's only a proposal. Comments are open until about 19 Jan, there's likely to be litigation, and the selloff hit the whole sector regardless of how exposed each company is.

**Bear case.** Repayments reaching back to 2018 are a liability that's hard to size, and UnitedHealth's medical-records and coding business, Optum, is exactly what this kind of audit targets. Political pressure on the company is high on several fronts.

**Questions for the deep dive.** How does UnitedHealth's coding intensity (how much its patients' recorded diagnoses raise payments) compare with its peers'? What has it set aside for audit risk? How did the earlier court rulings reason?

## 5. Palo Alto Networks (PANW): does AI-found software flaws mean more security spending?

**Why now.** Fiscal Q1 revenue [rose 39% to $3.44bn](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001327567/000132756726000030/ex991q127earningsrelease.htm). Annual recurring revenue from its newer security products rose 66% to $9.73bn, and full-year guidance was raised to $14.52–14.57bn. The results came a day after the Java4Shell alert, a flaw that the researchers say AI found in an hour for under $200.

**Bull case.** When finding vulnerabilities becomes cheap for attackers, companies' security budgets have to grow. Customers are also consolidating onto one large vendor's platform instead of many point products.

**Bear case.** Much of the growth was bought: the CyberArk acquisition was [paid for mostly in stock](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/ry2dd4eozg), which diluted existing shareholders. GAAP operating income [fell to $96m from $309m](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001327567/000132756726000030/ex991q127earningsrelease.htm), and another acquisition (Applied Compute) is coming.

**Questions for the deep dive.** What is organic growth excluding CyberArk? How large is stock-based compensation relative to free cash flow? Are customers actually consolidating vendors, or only being sold bundles?
