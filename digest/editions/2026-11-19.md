## 1. Gulf: renewed strikes; Hormuz re-priced

**What happened.** On 17 Nov the US [struck an IRGC missile site on Qeshm Island](https://gulfnews.com/world/mena/middle-east-war-iran-attacks-bahrain-kuwait-drone-threats-keep-gulf-on-high-alert-as-us-hits-irans-qeshm-1.500751444), calling it self-defense. That evening an ADNOC tanker was attacked in the strait (one crew member missing, 11 injured); the attacker hasn't been identified. Iranian drones targeted bases in Kuwait and Bahrain, and were intercepted. IEA head Fatih Birol [said there is no "credible pathway"](https://cnbc.com/2026/11/18/iea-strait-hormuz-energy-risk.html) back to pre-war normality, even though Gulf crude exports recovered to over 80% of pre-war levels in October. Brent was around $92.

**Background.** Before the war, Hormuz carried about a fifth of the world's oil, and the pipelines that bypass it carry only part of that volume. IEA members must hold 90 days of net imports in emergency stocks, which cushioned this year's shock.

**Why it matters.** Oil is flowing again, but insurers, importers and lenders now price in a second closure. That shows up as higher insurance, bigger stockpiles (the Philippines and Thailand are building fuel buffers), and more bypass pipelines. The Houthis [targeted one of those bypass pipelines](https://www.justsecurity.org/165038/early-edition-november-18-2026/) this week.

*Confidence:* the strikes are confirmed. Damage claims come from the parties involved. · *Watch:* tanker insurance rates; the IEA's December report.

## 2. The ICJ finds Myanmar committed genocide

**What happened.** The International Court of Justice [ruled 9–6](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/myanmar-genocide-rohingyas-un-top-court/) that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya and failed to prosecute those responsible. It [ordered restitution and compensation](https://www.outlookindia.com/international/icj-myanmar-genocidal-intent-rohingya-genocide-ruling), with a report due in two years. Myanmar [denies the charge](https://www.jpost.com/international/article-915430).

**Background.** The ICJ rules on states, not individuals. The Gambia could bring the case because every party to the Genocide Convention owes its obligations to all the others. The hard part is proving intent to destroy a group. In 2007 and 2015 the Court set that bar so high that no state was found to have committed genocide. The Court can't enforce its rulings itself.

**Why it matters.** *Supporters:* the Convention is now usable, and patterns of mass violence can meet the intent standard. *The six dissenters:* the majority loosened both the definition of "destruction" and the standard of evidence, which risks making "genocide" a routine political charge. The ruling will matter in other pending cases and in decisions on arms sales and sanctions.

*Confidence:* the holding and the vote are confirmed by multiple outlets. · *Watch:* Myanmar's response (it is already [reviewing its ASEAN ties](https://www.justsecurity.org/165038/early-edition-november-18-2026/)); any Security Council action.

## 3. Central banks split on whether to raise rates into an energy shock

**What happened.** The [Bank of England raised its rate to 4%](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy-summary-and-minutes/2026/november-2026) by 8–1, its first move since December 2025, citing Middle East energy prices. The [RBA raised to 4.85%](https://www.livewiremarkets.com/wires/rba-hikes-interest-rate-to-4-85-but-a-split-vote-hints-the-end-may-be-near) by 6–3, its highest since 2008, and the [Czech central bank to 4%](https://www.cnb.cz/en/cnb-news/press-releases/CNB-increases-interest-rates-00025/) by 4–3. [Mexico cut](https://mexiconewsdaily.com/business/bucking-expectations-banxico-lowers-interest-rate-despite-some-inflation-concerns/) to 6.25% by 3–2. The [Fed held at 3.75–4.00%](https://www.newsquawk.com/headlines/primer-fomc-minutes-due-wednesday-1900gmt1400est) with two dissents. Gold has [fallen more than 25%](https://aa.com.tr/en/world/morning-briefing-nov-18-2026/4128568) since the war began.

**Background.** Higher rates cool inflation caused by too much spending. An oil shock is different: it raises prices while slowing the economy, so the textbook advice is to wait it out unless it causes *second-round effects*, such as wage demands and price-setting built on expectations of more inflation.

**Why it matters.** *Hawks:* expectations are fragile after years of high inflation, so stopping now would cost more later. *Doves (including the dissenters):* rate rises don't produce oil, they only add a recession. The close votes show how evenly the argument is balanced. Gold is falling because cash and bonds now pay a return after inflation, and gold pays nothing.

*Confidence:* confirmed against the banks' own releases. · *Watch:* BoE on 17 Dec; [Fed Governor Cook's dismissal hearing](https://www.justsecurity.org/165038/early-edition-november-18-2026/) on 14 Dec.

## 4. Java4Shell: an AI-found flaw and a patching rush

**What happened.** The security firm Calif [found a remote-code-execution flaw](https://blog.qualys.com/vulnerabilities-threat-research/2026/11/17/how-to-find-mitigate-and-patch-the-critical-java4shell-flaw-cve-2026-114938-now) in Java, CVE-2026-114938. It says AI found the flaw in about an hour, for under $200. It affects Java 17.0.6+ on certain x86-64 processors. Oracle and Amazon shipped fixes on 17 Nov. After outages at Microsoft, the FAA and banks, US officials [said there's "zero evidence" of an attack](https://www.justsecurity.org/165038/early-edition-november-18-2026/) and pointed to rushed patching.

**Background.** Java runs much of the back-end software in banking, aviation and government. A fix only takes effect when each running Java program restarts. That made Log4Shell (2021) take months to clear.

**Why it matters.** AI has made finding serious flaws fast and cheap, but fixing them still means restarting live systems, which is slow and risky. How quickly systems can be safely restarted is becoming a security measure in its own right.

*Confidence:* the CVE details are primary. The patching explanation is the US government's own assessment. · *Watch:* fixes from other Java vendors; any confirmed exploitation.

## 5. Trump, Xi and Putin meet as a three

**What happened.** On the APEC sidelines in Shenzhen, the three leaders met together for the first time. [Chinese state media report](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/liveblog/2026/11/19/apec-live-trump-xi-and-putin-to-meet-in-historic-three-way-china-summit) a "peace declaration" with a commitment to "stable energy flows," and mention nuclear talks. No text has been published.

**Background.** Nuclear arms control has always been two-sided, first US–Soviet and then US–Russia. China has refused to join, citing its far smaller arsenal. A three-way process would end that refusal.

**Why it matters.** *Optimistic:* a standing channel among the three largest nuclear powers reduces the risk of miscalculation. *Skeptical:* making China host and broker increases Beijing's leverage, and a declaration nobody can read commits nobody to anything.

*Confidence:* the meeting is confirmed. Its content comes only from Chinese state media. · *Watch:* US or Russian readouts; the G20 in Florida on 14–15 Dec.

## Concept: the risk premium that outlasts the risk

After a failure, prices don't return to where they were once things work again. Markets add a premium for the chance it happens again, which shows up in insurance, inventories, lending rates and bypass investments. Hormuz flows can fully recover while the system still costs more to run. **For any "back to normal" headline, ask whether the cost of insuring against a repeat has come down.**
